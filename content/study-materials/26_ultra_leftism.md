# Opposing Ultra-leftism, The Marxist Method

*By Steve Leigh*

> Marxism is essentially the full completion of class struggle.
>
> Revolutionary organizations need to steer clear of reformism and
> opportunism. However, there is another danger, recruiting on the basis
> of ultra-leftism.

For Marx, socialism was the self-emancipation of the working class. John
Molyneux called Marxism “the theory of international proletarian
revolution.” These basic statements have implications that some Marxists
have ignored or misunderstood.

Marxism is essentially the full completion of class struggle. Instead of
just resisting or modifying exploitation, Marxism eliminates it. Instead
of just fighting the ruling class, communism abolishes all social
classes.

For Marx, communism was not just a doctrine, but the real movement of
the working class. This led Marx to reject utopianism. It led him to
call for communist involvement in the class struggle instead of
criticizing it from the outside. He said of communists, “They do not set
up any sectarian principles of their own. They have no interests
separate and apart from those of the proletariat as a whole.”

The distinguishing thing about communists is that: “ In the national
struggles of the proletarians of the different countries, they point out
and bring to the front the common interests of the entire proletariat,
independently of all nationality. 2. In the various stages of
development which the struggle of the working class against the
bourgeoisie has to pass through, they always and everywhere represent
the interests of the movement as a whole.”

And

“The theoretical conclusions of the Communists are in no way based on
ideas or principles that have been invented, or discovered, by this or
that would-be universal reformer.

They merely express, in general terms, actual relations springing from
an existing class struggle, from a historical movement going on under
our very eyes.”

This approach by Marx was based on the idea that “capitalism first of
all creates its own gravediggers, the working class”. In other words,
the tendency of the class struggle is toward communism.

## Reformism

As history later showed, Marx had underestimated the potential strength
of reformism. The class struggle could be diverted from its
revolutionary potential as Rosa Luxemburg laid out in *Reform or
Revolution*.

Lenin drew organizational conclusions from the need to fight reformism.
By 1912, he had come to the conclusion that an exclusively revolutionary
organization was needed to overcome reformism. He developed the theory
of the revolutionary socialist vanguard party: the most revolutionary
workers must unite in order to convince the whole working class to take
power. In fact, this same conclusion had been briefly reached by Marx in
1849. He expressed this in the “*Address to the Communist League*.”

## Did Leninism Complete or Reject Marx's Approach to Class Struggle?

The *Communist Manifesto* was a document produced by the Communist
League. Even as Marx urged communists to participate in class struggle,
he understood the need for an organization of communists within the
class struggle. He called for communists to win the movement to
internationalism and the ultimate goal of communism. While the
*Manifesto* rejected “sects”, it did support communist organization
within the struggle. His rejection of *separate* principles by
communists was a rejection of utopianism, not a rejection of political
argument within the working-class movement.

He said:

“The *Communists disdain to conceal their views and aims. They openly
declare that their ends can be attained only by the forcible overthrow
of all existing social conditions*.”

As the communist movement developed it came to more clarity on the
principles that were needed to complete the class struggle into
communism. These for the most part were already explicit or implicit in
Marx's writing: confrontation and militancy, opposition to racism,
sexism etc., the use of the united front method, working class
independence from capitalist politics and parties, internationalism,
revolution etc.

Over time communists understood the need to more directly confront
reformism in the working-class movement. Lenin's call for a
revolutionary vanguard party grew from this understanding. Lenin's
organizational ideas were in line with Marx's overall approach to the
working-class movement. In *What Is to Be Done*, he called for
“educating spontaneity”. Lenin made this clear in *Left-wing Communism,
An Infantile Disorder*.

## Implications for Today

While aiming to build a Leninist Vanguard Party, revolutionaries today
should adhere to Marx's overall method. The revolutionary organization
needs to be part of the working-class struggle. It wants to see that
struggle succeed. It supports the aims and ends of that struggle. It is
in the main a critical supporter of the working-class movement.

There is an important strategic reason to take this stance: Throughout
working-class history, reactionaries and even liberals denounce
communists as “outside agitators”. They claim that communists are not
legitimate members of the movement. Communists have to fight for their
right to be activists in the movement. To defend their participation,
communists have to show that they support the aims of the movement. The
way to win people to communist strategies, tactics and politics is for
communists to be seen as fighting alongside other activists. The
ultra-left attitude of primarily denouncing the movement rather than
giving it critical support, leads to voluntary self-isolation. It
undercuts the possibility of winning workers to communism. It does the
work of reactionaries for them.

If the working class movement, the struggle against exploitation, is the
force that will bring communism, communists must be part of that
movement.

## Reform Movements

This applies to movements for reforms that express the interests of the
working-class: movements for equality and liberation, against
imperialism, against authoritarianism etc. Revolutionaries need to go
into these movements as militant but critical supporters, even if they
oppose the politics of the leadership of those movements. Marxists want
these movements to succeed. Marxists criticize these movements mainly
over their strategies, tactics and leadership politics. This means that
revolutionaries join these movements positively. Marxists want as many
people involved as possible and try to build these movements even while
critical of their leadership. Of course, as Marx said in the Manifesto,
communists “disdain to hide their politics.” Critical support means
openness about criticism. As British Marxist Tony Cliff put it “We are
*with* and against the movements.”

## Completion, not Fundamental Opposition to the Movement

Flowing from Marx's method in relation to movements, revolutionaries try
to “*radicalize movements from within, rather than pooh pooh them from
without*” (Engels). This means being fundamentally with the movement
even with their criticism of it. It flows from Marx's idea that
communism is the ultimate completion of the class struggle. Though it
can be diverted by reformism, when the movement reaches its logical
goals, it reaches communism.

## Ultra-Leftism

The basic idea of ultra-leftism is that the working-class movement does
not have a fundamental trajectory towards communism. Ultra-leftists
think that the movement needs to be rejected and replaced by an
authentic communist movement. Ultra-leftists opposed unions that were
under reformist leadership. They wanted to form separate organizations
outside of the dominant working-class organizations. They set up red
unions only made up of communists instead of contesting for leadership
within existing unions. Instead of fighting reformist bureaucrats for
the allegiance of the workers, they let the reformists dominate the
union's politics by withdrawing from the unions.

## The Theory of the Offensive

One manifestation of ultra-leftism was the “theory of the offensive”.
This was the idea that the working class had to be shocked into
activity. In 1921, the Communist Party of Germany determined that a
general strike was necessary. Instead of arguing for and winning the
majority of workers to this idea, they declared the general strike and
tried to force workers off work. This resulted in physical battles, mass
arrests, and even death of workers on both sides. It was a major
set-back. It in turn resulted in the failure to organize an insurrection
in 1923 when the working class was ready for it. Ultra-leftism made the
CP skittish about waging a battle when it was possible.

## Applications Today

The Left in the U.S. is tiny. It needs to grow significantly before it
can have much impact on class struggle. While revolutionary
organizations need to grow, they must grow on a solid basis. They could
open their doors to supporters of the Democratic Party and likely become
much bigger. The DSA method seems superficially to work since it has
become an organization of tens of thousands. However, it is not
revolutionary. It has become an obstacle to class struggle as it
supports the second major imperialist party.

Revolutionary organizations need to steer clear of reformism and
opportunism. However, there is another danger, recruiting on the basis
of ultra-leftism. This can also create a house of cards. Recruiting on
this basis may lead to increases in membership. The problem is that this
will prevent growth beyond a certain point. Ultra-leftism will deter
serious revolutionaries from joining. If an organization does grow based
on ultra-leftism, it will become an obstacle to struggle in the future.
